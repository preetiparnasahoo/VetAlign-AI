1. The problem

A Havildar retiring after fifteen years has run a battalion's stores and transport and trained forty jawans. On a civilian job portal that reads as nothing, because the words do not match: recruiters search for inventory management, fleet coordination and team training, not stores, convoy and jawans. Most ex-servicemen retire in their late thirties or forties and need a second career. [VERIFY a sourced figure before using it, e.g. annual retirements from the Directorate General of Resettlement]. Writing a civilian CV means typing in a second language, and paid CV writers charge for it. Generic AI writers inflate rank into titles that were never held and invent certificates.

2. Who it helps

Retiring and retired Indian Army, Navy and Air Force personnel looking for civilian work, especially those more comfortable speaking Hindi than typing English. Secondary users: resettlement counsellors and ex-servicemen association volunteers who help them.

3. The solution

The veteran speaks for 30–90 seconds in Hindi or mixed Hindi-English. Three steps later they have a civilian profile and up to three matching open roles they can act on today.

 Voice note (Hindi)  ─►  Gemini: transcript + service, rank, years, trade, work city
                          (each field must quote the speaker's exact words)
                     ─►  Veteran reviews, corrects and approves
                     ─►  Gemini: civilian skills, each cited to a numbered fact,
                          8-word headline, 3-sentence summary, top-3 job IDs
                     ─►  App: rejects unknown/closed/uncited jobs; copies employer,
                          link and contact from the job list (never from the AI)
                     ─►  Matched roles with reason + gaps · editable profile · download

4. Why AI is essential

  • Hindi speech → structured facts. Gemini transcribes code-switched Hindi and pulls out rank, years and trade. It tells "15 साल" (years of service) apart from "40 जवान" (team size), which a form or keyword matcher cannot do.
  • Military → civilian translation. A mapping in the prompt (stores → inventory management, convoy → fleet & logistics, training jawans → team training & supervision, guard duty → security operations, signals → telecom/IT support) produces specific skills instead of "disciplined, hard-working".
  • Matching by skills, not titles. A "Havildar" never appears in a job ad. Gemini compares the evidenced skills with each role's required skills and explains the fit and the gaps.
  • Guard-rails in code, not trust. Every skill must cite the veteran's own words. Job IDs not in the list, or matches citing no fact, are discarded. A defence licence is never presented as a civilian licence.

7. Limits and what breaks

  • The job list is small (13 fictional roles). It lives in a Google Sheet a non-technical volunteer can edit; employers offer roles through a Google Form, and nothing goes live until the volunteer marks it open. Proposed maintainer: an ex-servicemen association volunteer or Zila Sainik Welfare Office helper, about 15 minutes a week. Roles not checked for 45 days hide themselves; bad rows are skipped with a reason, never breaking the list.
  • Free-tier Gemini is sometimes busy; retry + backup model reduce, not remove, failures.
  • Marathi voice is not offered yet; real-accent testing is limited.
  • Advisory only: it does not certify equivalence or decide eligibility. The veteran reviews every line.
